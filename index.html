Privacy & method
  • Aggregate only. The map never shows an individual access point, BSSID or device. Each hexagon (~450 m) holds two daily counts: baseline APs still present and newly seen APs.
  • Small cells are suppressed. Cells with fewer than 25 baseline APs are dropped when the dataset is built.
  • Opt-outs are honored. Networks named _nomap or _optout are discarded on ingest, along with randomized and phone-hotspot MACs.
  • No movement tracking. The paper shows how the same data can follow a travel router across borders. This tool deliberately doesn't link APs across days or places.
  • Detection. The control is the median Philadelphia cell, playing the role of the paper's Tel Aviv comparison group. A cell is flagged when its surviving share of baseline APs falls below that control by at least the minimum drop, the binomial z-score clears the threshold, and at least 8 APs are involved. Influxes use the same test on newly seen APs, with a Poisson model. Adjacent flagged cells are grouped into incidents, followed day to day, and kept once they last 3 days.
  • Data source. This demo uses simulated counts. Real counts need observations you are authorized to collect (see pipeline/README.md). This project doesn't scrape Apple's positioning service.
